Tooth Extraction Consent — Summary

This summary is for reading before you give consent. Actual consent takes place at the clinic: after your dentist explains the treatment and answers your questions, you sign the original consent form.

1

Why the tooth needs to come out

Extraction removes a tooth that cannot be saved because of decay, gum disease, a fractured tooth or root, or inflammation of the nerve or root tip, as well as impacted wisdom teeth.

Left in place, such a tooth can cause repeated pain and infection, and inflammation can spread to neighboring teeth and bone.

Simple extraction

The tooth is removed as it is.

Surgical extraction

The gum is opened, the tooth is sectioned or bone is trimmed, and the site is stitched (impacted teeth, curved or fused roots).

2

Good to know about the procedure

The condition of the tooth and bone can only be fully seen during extraction.

  • Depending on what is found, the method may change or additional treatment may be needed.
  • In an emergency, measures needed for your safety come first.
  • If needed, we may refer you to a hospital with more advanced care.
3

Other treatment options

Root canal + crown

Depending on the tooth, it may be saved with root canal treatment and a crown.

Gum treatment

Periodontal treatment may be tried to keep the tooth.

No extraction

Pain and infection may recur, inflammation may spread to neighboring teeth and the jaw, or a cyst may form.

If the tooth is badly broken or much of the bone is lost, success cannot be guaranteed, and pain or infection may return.

4

Please tell us

For a safe extraction, let us know in advance if you have any of the following:

DiabetesHigh blood pressure / heart diseaseOsteoporosis medicationDrug / anesthetic allergyBlood thinners / bleeding disorderSmokingHead & neck radiation therapyChemotherapy / immunosuppressantsPregnancy (or possible)Other conditions / medications
5

Possible complications

During and after extraction

Pain, swelling, bleeding, bruising, infection, opening of stitches, pain at the injection site, brief dizziness or palpitations, scrapes or blisters on the lips or mouth corners (usually heal within days), limited mouth opening or jaw joint pain, sharp bone fragments coming through the gum, side effects of prescribed medicines (antibiotics, painkillers).

Dry socket

If the blood clot is lost and bone is exposed, severe pain may start 3–5 days after extraction, and several visits for cleaning may be needed.

Nerve injury

Especially with lower molars and wisdom teeth: numbness or altered sensation of the lower lip, chin, tongue or gums, or a change in taste. Most recover within weeks, but it may last over 6 months or, rarely, not recover.

Teeth, nearby structures, upper jaw

Root fracture (part of a root may be left if removing it near a nerve is riskier), damage to neighboring teeth or crowns, fracture of the socket bone, sinus perforation or sinusitis after upper molar extraction (rarely a root enters the sinus, requiring surgery or an ENT specialist), swallowing or inhaling instruments or tooth fragments.

Rare but serious

  • Bleeding that is hard to stop or starts again days later (more likely with blood thinners).
  • Stopping blood thinners on your own raises the risk of stroke and heart attack. Whether to stop is decided with the doctor who prescribed them.
  • With a history of osteoporosis medication, the site may heal poorly and, rarely, jawbone necrosis (MRONJ) may occur.
  • Subcutaneous emphysema (air trapped in tissue causing swelling), jawbone infection (osteomyelitis).
  • Lower jaw fracture, bleeding in the floor of the mouth, spreading infection (sepsis), anaphylaxis (shock) — emergency care and hospital transfer needed.
6

What we ask of you

  • Bite firmly on the gauze as instructed, and do not touch the site with your tongue, fingers or a straw.
  • Do not smoke — it slows healing and raises the risk of infection. Follow prescriptions and your dentist's instructions.
  • Tell us right away if bleeding does not stop or pain or swelling gets worse, and if your health or medications change.

By signing the consent form, you confirm that you received and understood the explanation and agree to the extraction of your own free will.

The attending dentist gives the explanation and performs the main part of the surgery; if needed, other dentists at the clinic may take part in some steps. If the patient is a minor or cannot understand the document, a representative signs.
