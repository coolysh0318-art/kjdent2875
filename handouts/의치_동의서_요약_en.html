Denture Treatment Consent — Summary

This summary is for reading before you give consent. Actual consent takes place at the clinic: after your dentist explains the treatment and answers your questions, you sign the original consent form.

1

Purpose and types

Dentures replace missing teeth and surrounding tissue with artificial teeth and a denture base to restore chewing, speech and appearance. Unlike natural teeth, they are not fixed in bone but rest on the gums, which sets basic limits.

Complete denture

The gums and palate take all of the biting force.

Partial denture

Clasps hook onto remaining teeth, which share the force with the gums.

Implant overdenture

Clips onto implants in the jawbone for better retention (implant surgery is explained and consented separately).

Impressions→Wax try-in (check the look)→Delivery→Several adjustments

A denture is not finished on the day it is fitted; it is completed through several adjustments.

2

Important to know

Even a well-made denture has these limits:

  • Chewing power is less than natural teeth. Biting with the front teeth is hard, and hard, tough or sticky foods are difficult.
  • Getting used to it takes time and several adjustments. Sore spots on the gums are a normal part of the process.
  • The jawbone keeps shrinking, so the denture becomes loose over time and needs relining or remaking.
  • Changes after the wax try-in require a new denture.
3

Other treatment options

  • Implants — Bone takes the force, so chewing is close to natural teeth with little bulk, but surgery, more time and cost are needed, and bone or general health may prevent it or require bone grafting.
  • Fixed bridge — Feels natural with little bulk, but the teeth on both sides must be trimmed, and it is not possible for large gaps or when the back teeth are missing.
  • No treatment — Remaining teeth drift, opposing teeth over-erupt and bone keeps shrinking, making dentures or implants harder later; chewing, speech and nutrition worsen and facial appearance may change.
4

Possible complications

Function and adaptation

A bulky or foreign feeling, more saliva, speech changes, gagging (severe gagging may prevent wearing it), reduced taste and temperature sense (an upper denture covers the palate, so hot food can burn it), teeth that differ from what you expected in shade, shape or arrangement, and changes in facial appearance. Adaptation varies greatly; some people never become comfortable despite adjustments.

Gums, tissue and retention

Sore spots and ulcers (may recur), looseness (especially lower complete dentures, with low bone or dry mouth), yeast-related denture stomatitis (higher risk if worn at night), allergy to materials, gum overgrowth from ill-fitting dentures; non-healing sores or patches need checking. Minor injuries during impressions; side effects of anesthesia.

Changes over time

Bone loss cannot be prevented, so even a good denture gradually loosens and hurts and needs repair or remaking. Clasped teeth may become sensitive or loose and prone to decay and wear; if one is lost, the denture must be modified or remade.

Implant overdentures

Retention improves, but the gums still take part of the force, so the limits above still apply and adjustment and relining are needed. Caps and O-rings are wear parts that must be replaced at a cost; peri-implantitis or failed integration can cause implant loss, requiring the denture to be modified or remade.

Rare but serious

  • Allergy to medicines or materials, anaphylaxis (shock) — emergency care and hospital transfer needed.

6

What we ask of you

  • Come to all scheduled adjustments, checks and check-ups.
  • At the wax try-in (trying a temporary wax denture), check the shade, size, shape and arrangement of the teeth and your facial appearance carefully.
  • Clean your denture and mouth daily, and take the denture out at night to rest your gums.
  • Tell us about pain, looseness or other problems, or if your health or medications change.

By signing the consent form, you confirm that you received and understood the explanation — especially the limits of chewing power, the need for adaptation and adjustments, relining or remaking due to bone loss, and remaking for changes after the wax try-in — and agree to denture treatment of your own free will.

The attending dentist gives the explanation and performs the main part of the surgery; if needed, other dentists at the clinic may take part in some steps. If the patient is a minor or cannot understand the document, a representative signs.
