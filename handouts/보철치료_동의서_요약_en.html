Prosthetic Treatment Consent — Summary

This summary is for reading before you give consent. Actual consent takes place at the clinic: after your dentist explains the treatment and answers your questions, you sign the original consent form.

1

Purpose and types of treatment

Restores the shape, chewing and speech function of damaged or missing teeth and, when needed, improves their appearance.

Crown

Covers and protects a weakened tooth

Bridge

Replaces a missing tooth, anchored to the teeth on either side

Inlay

A custom-made piece fills the prepared area

Veneer

A thin shell bonded to a lightly trimmed front tooth

Tooth preparation→Impression (temporary tooth)→Fabrication & bonding→Bite adjustment

Depending on the tooth, root canal treatment, gum treatment or a post and core may be needed first, or the plan may change.

2

Good to know beforehand

Tooth structure removed for a restoration cannot be restored.

  • How much is trimmed depends on the type and the tooth.
  • When the goal is mainly cosmetic, as with veneers, keeping your teeth as they are is also an option.
  • Results depend on your teeth and gums, so the final restoration may not exactly match the preview; remaking it for cosmetic reasons may require more trimming.
3

Other treatment options

  • Direct filling (resin) — Less trimming and done in one visit, but limited strength, lifespan and color for large damage.
  • Inlay vs crown — An inlay removes less tooth but protects a weak tooth less; a crown protects more but removes more.
  • Instead of veneers — Resin, whitening or orthodontics trim less or not at all, but the improvement and how long it lasts differ.
  • Instead of a bridge — An implant spares the neighboring teeth but needs surgery, time and cost; a denture chews less well and feels bulky.
  • No treatment — Decay, fracture, pain, infection, tooth drifting and bone loss may progress, making later treatment harder (for cosmetic goals, teeth simply stay as they are).
4

Possible complications

Tooth trimming and nerve

Sensitivity or pain to hot and cold may occur. It usually fades, but if it persists or worsens a root canal may be needed; the nerve may also die or the tooth discolor after placement, requiring a root canal (hard to predict in advance).

Fracture and loosening

The tooth, a bridge abutment or the root may fracture, requiring re-restoration, a root canal or extraction (root-canal-treated teeth are weaker); restorations may chip, wear or come off, requiring repair, re-cementing or remaking.

Gums and secondary decay

Gum inflammation or recession at the margin (exposed edge, sensitivity), decay under the restoration (may be painless in root-canal-treated teeth and found late), staining or gaps at the margin, food trapping. With poor care, a bridge can lose its abutment teeth and need to be remade.

Bite, adaptation, appearance, other

Bite adjustment, discomfort when chewing or jaw joint pain, a foreign-body feeling or speech change, color or shape differences from natural teeth (veneers may differ in shade or show their edge), loose, broken or sensitive temporaries, damage to neighboring tooth surfaces, scrapes or burns to lips, cheeks or tongue, injection pain, bruising or dizziness (rarely lasting numbness). Restorations have a limited lifespan that depends on hygiene, habits and care.

Rare but serious

  • Allergy to dental materials or medicines; rarely anaphylaxis (shock) — emergency care and hospital transfer needed.
  • Swallowing or inhaling instruments or materials, which may need medical care.

Please tell us

6

What we ask of you

  • Come for regular check-ups.
  • Tell us right away about sensitivity, pain, or a loose or broken restoration, or if your health or medications change.
  • Clean daily with a toothbrush, floss and interdental brushes — food easily gets trapped under a bridge.
  • Avoid hard or sticky foods and habits that strain restorations, such as grinding or clenching.

Fees and warranty: root canal treatment, gum treatment or a post and core needed before or with the restoration are separate treatments with separate fees, as are repair, remaking or re-cementing — though these may be free or partly covered within the warranty period and conditions. Amounts, refunds and warranty terms will be explained to you separately.

By signing the consent form, you confirm that you received and understood the explanation and agree to prosthetic treatment of your own free will.

The attending dentist gives the explanation and performs the main part of the surgery; if needed, other dentists at the clinic may take part in some steps. If the patient is a minor or cannot understand the document, a representative signs.
